Original GEP2 scores were reproduced directly from frozen sparse counts,
available strict-one-to-one ortholog members and fixed ROI metadata. The maximum
per-bin reproduction error was 2.22e-16 in each capture. Original coordinates and
all protected original inputs, including ROI and image hashes, were unchanged.
This verifies the computation and geometry, not the anatomical diagnosis.

GEP2 score was positively associated with original bin UMI. Descriptive Spearman
rho in all QC bins was 0.4925 in CTR and 0.3856 in cKO; in NP/AF cores combined
it was 0.5607 and 0.5435, respectively. No bin-level P value was calculated.

One fixed-count analysis was predeclared before viewing its outcomes: eligible
core bins had at least 100 original molecules; one exact sample of 100 molecules
without replacement across all genes was drawn per bin, seed 20261022. The same
22 available strict-one-to-one GEP2 members, including zero expression, defined
the score before and after sampling. The available genes and ROIs were not
retuned. At least five retained bins in each compartment were required for a
descriptive disc pair, and at least three paired discs per capture were required.

Retention was imbalanced. CTR NP retained 173/174 bins (99.4%) and AF retained
42/57 (73.7%); cKO NP retained 61/62 (98.4%) and AF retained 27/69 (39.1%).
Original eligible UMI ranges were CTR NP 118–3,971, CTR AF 100–706, cKO NP
109–1,595 and cKO AF 100–237; after sampling every retained bin had exactly 100
UMIs. Thus the fixed library size is directly comparable, but eligibility
selects a much smaller and potentially unrepresentative subset of low-depth AF.

Four CTR and three cKO disc pairs met coverage safeguards. cKO_BL retained only
three AF bins and was excluded from adequate-pair summaries with its original
and sampled measurements retained in the full table. The original NP-minus-AF
contrast was positive in all eight discs. Among the same seven adequately
covered discs, the equal-disc mean contrast on the original eligible bins was
0.17949 and fell to 0.03170 after 100-UMI sampling; six of seven paired contrasts
remained positive. The adequately covered cKO_TR pair reversed. These results
support a depth-sensitive, substantially attenuated descriptive spatial trend,
not uniformly preserved compartment enrichment. This single draw supplies no
sampling uncertainty interval, and the deliberately limited check cannot
separate every source of composition or technical bias.

Original candidate detection was not depth-corrected. FN1 was detectable in NP
and AF cores of all eight discs. ITGB1 was detected in seven NP and one AF core.
PRSS23 was detected in one bin of one CTR NP core with one UMI, and in no AF
core. CCN2 was detectable in eight AF cores and two NP cores. Sparse or zero
detected expression is restricted to these measured cores and depth. It does
not establish absence in the tissue, species or human disease. ITGB1 is an
integrin-complex member; its presence alone is not evidence of a complete
functional FN1 receptor complex or directional AF-to-NP signaling.

The eight provisional disc structures contain eight NP and sixteen bilateral
AF core polygons, with zero CEP regions. H&E geometry was frozen before program
or candidate measurements, then tested for compatible directions with
independent NP Krt19/T/Krt8/Acan and AF Chad/Clec3a panels. This is structural
marker compatibility, not expert histopathological validation. GEP5 originally
showed the opposite compartment trend (AF greater than NP in eight discs), a
cross-layer difference that is retained rather than relabeled as NP validation.

The two captures are from an adult six-month conditional-knockout mouse context,
one capture per genotype, and genotype is completely confounded with capture.
Released individual animal-to-disc identifiers are unavailable. Bins are
technical units and discs remain nested in capture. No animal-level or genotype
P value, human spatial replication, spatial adjacency, receptor activation,
molecular transfer, or causal communication claim follows from this analysis.
